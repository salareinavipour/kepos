# Introduction to Philosophy of Science (Core Course)

## Description

A survey of central themes in 20th- and 21st-century philosophy of science,
moving from logical positivism and Popper's falsificationism through Kuhn's
philosophy of science, feminist philosophy of science, and up to current
debates on science policy and open science. Recurring topics include
explanation, confirmation, reduction, pluralism, values in science, concept
formation, models, and experimentation — plus how philosophy of science
relates to the history and social study of science.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

1. Give a broad overview of topics and debates in 20th/21st century
   philosophy of science.
2. Explain key concepts (observation, explanation, values in science, etc.).
3. Situate those concepts within their major debates (e.g. DN-explanation
   vs. mechanistic explanation, realism vs. antirealism, value-free vs.
   value-laden science).
4. Work with a range of classic and recent texts and authors in the field.
5. Read and analyze philosophical texts with some competence.

## Recommended background reading

- Barker, Gillian & Kitcher, Philipp (2014), *Philosophy of Science: A New
  Introduction*. Oxford University Press.
- Cover, J.A., Curd, Martin, and Pincock, Chris (eds.) (2012), *Philosophy
  of Science: The Central Issues*, 2nd ed. Norton.
- Godfrey-Smith, Peter (2021), *Theory and Reality: An Introduction to the
  Philosophy of Science*, 2nd ed. University of Chicago Press.
- Potochnik, Angela; Colombo, Matteo; Wright, Cory (2019), *Recipes for
  Science: An Introduction to Scientific Methods and Reasoning*. Routledge.
