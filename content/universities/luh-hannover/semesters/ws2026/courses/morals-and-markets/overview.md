# Morals and Markets (MA Seminar)

## Description

Economics is also a deeply moral discipline. The seminar explores the
interface of economics, ethics, and political philosophy: the moral foundations
of markets, markets and freedom, the distribution of economic goods, and the
moral limits of commodification. Guiding questions include whether market
exchange is automatically legitimate, whether markets build or erode virtues,
and how economic institutions shape our ideas of justice and the common good.
Readings mix economists and philosophers.

## Learning goals

By the end, students should be able to: know the core debates linking moral
questions to markets; reconstruct their main concepts and positions; discuss
them critically; and state their own position in speech and writing.

## Background literature

- Hausman, McPherson, and Satz (2017), *Economic Analysis, Moral Philosophy,
  and Public Policy*, 3rd ed., Cambridge University Press (several chapters are
  assigned).
- Reiss (2013), *Philosophy of Economics: A Contemporary Introduction*, Routledge.
- Sen (1988), *On Ethics & Economics*, Blackwell.
