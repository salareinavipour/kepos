# Reading list — The Ethics of Artificial Intelligence

## Course textbook

- Liao, S. Matthew (ed.) (2020), *Ethics of Artificial Intelligence*, Oxford
  University Press. Most required readings are chapters from this book; see
  `../sessions/` for the pages assigned each week.

## Additional readings (distributed separately)

- Neil M. Richards, “Freedom,” in *Why Privacy Matters* (Oxford University
  Press, 2021), pp. 131–163.

## Optional readings (outside the textbook)

- Thomson, Judith Jarvis (1985), “The Trolley Problem,” *The Yale Law Journal* 94(6).
- Awad et al. (2018), “The Moral Machine Experiment,” *Nature* 563.
- O’Neil, Cathy (2016), *Weapons of Math Destruction*, ch. 5.
- Rawls, John (1999), *A Theory of Justice*, rev. ed.
- Nissenbaum, Helen (2004), “Privacy as Contextual Integrity,” *Washington Law Review* 79(1).
- Van Parijs, Philippe (2004), “Basic Income,” *Politics & Society* 32(1).
- Sparrow, Robert (2007), “Killer Robots,” *Journal of Applied Philosophy* 24(1).
- ICRC (2021), *ICRC Position on Autonomous Weapon Systems*.
- Bostrom, Nick (2012), “The Superintelligent Will,” *Minds and Machines* 22.
- Krakovna et al. (2020), “Specification Gaming: The Flip Side of AI Ingenuity,” DeepMind.
- Aristotle, *Nicomachean Ethics*, Books II and VI.
- Vallor, Shannon (2016), *Technology and the Virtues*, OUP, pp. 17–34.
