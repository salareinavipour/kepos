# Reading list — Values in Science

Required texts are listed per session in `../sessions/`. Texts below are optional further reading.

## Overviews of the field

- Elliott and Richards (eds.) (2026), *The Routledge Handbook of Values and Science* [Parts 5 and 6].
- Elliott and Richards (eds.) (2017), *Exploring Inductive Risk: Case Studies of Values in Science*, OUP [Parts 3 and 4].
- Elliott and Steel (eds.) (2017), *Current Controversies in Values and Science*, Routledge [Part 5].

## General introductions

- Douglas (2009), *Science, Policy, and the Value-Free Ideal*, Pittsburgh.
- Elliott (2017), *A Tapestry of Values*, OUP.
- Elliott (2022), *Values in Science*, Cambridge Elements.

## Part 1: The value-free ideal

- Proctor (1991), *Value-Free Science? Purity and Power in Modern Knowledge*, Harvard (history of the ideal).
- Robbins (1935), *An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science*.
- Ayer (1936/1952), *Language, Truth and Logic*.
- Friedman (1953), “The Methodology of Positive Economics,” in *Essays in Positive Economics*.
- Lacey (1999), *Is Science Value Free?*
- Putnam (2002), *The Collapse of the Fact/Value Dichotomy*.
- Douglas (2009), *Science, Policy, and the Value-Free Ideal*, chs. 1 and 3.

## Part 2: Underdetermination

- Quine (1975), “On Empirically Equivalent Systems of the World,” *Erkenntnis* 9(3).
- Longino (1990), *Science as Social Knowledge*, ch. 3.
- Potter (1996), “Underdetermination Undeterred,” in *Feminism, Science, and the Philosophy of Science*.

## Part 2: Inductive risk

- Douglas (2000), “Inductive Risk and Values in Science,” *Philosophy of Science* 67(4).
- Wilholt (2024), “The Seriousness of Mistakes and the Benefits of Getting It Right,” *Kennedy Inst. Ethics J.* 34(4).
- Levi (1960), “Must the Scientist Make Value Judgments?” *J. Philosophy* 57(11).
- Hempel (1965), “Science and Human Values,” in *Aspects of Scientific Explanation*.
- Steel (2010), “Epistemic Values and the Argument from Inductive Risk,” *Philosophy of Science* 77(1).
- Betz (2013), “In Defence of the Value Free Ideal,” *Eur. J. Phil. Sci.* 3(2).
- John (2015a), “The Example of the IPCC Does Not Vindicate the Value Free Ideal,” *Eur. J. Phil. Sci.* 5(1).
- John (2015b), “Inductive Risk and the Contexts of Communication,” *Synthese* 192(1).
- de Melo-Martín and Intemann (2016), “The Risk of Using Inductive Risk to Challenge the Value-Free Ideal,” *Philosophy of Science* 83(4).
- Ward (2021), “On Value-Laden Science,” *Stud. Hist. Phil. Sci. A* 85.

## Part 3: Which values, and whose?

- Elliott (2011), “Direct and Indirect Roles for Values in Science,” *Philosophy of Science* 78(2).
- Elliott (2013), “Douglas on Values: From Indirect Roles to Multiple Goals,” *Stud. Hist. Phil. Sci. A* 44(3).
- Hicks (2014), “A New Direction for Science and Values,” *Synthese* 191(14).
- Intemann (2015), “Distinguishing Between Legitimate and Illegitimate Values in Climate Modeling,” *Eur. J. Phil. Sci.* 5(2).
- Kourany (2010), *Philosophy of Science after Feminism*, OUP.
- Wilholt (2009), “Bias and Values in Scientific Research,” *Stud. Hist. Phil. Sci.* 40(1).
- Kitcher (2011), *Science in a Democratic Society*.
- Hilligardt (2024), “Science as Public Service,” *Eur. J. Phil. Sci.* 14.
- Hilligardt and Wilholt (2026), “Science and Democracy,” in the *Routledge Handbook of Values and Science*.
- Le Bihan (2024), “How to Not Secure Public Trust in Science,” *Philosophy of Science* 91(5).
- John (2018), “Epistemic Trust and the Ethics of Science Communication,” *Social Epistemology* 32(2).
- Alexandrova (2018), “Can the Science of Well-Being Be Objective?” *BJPS* 69(2).

## Part 4: A new orthodoxy on the horizon?

- Lewens (2025), “Values as Evidence in the Sciences,” *Analysis*, anaf062.
- Brown (2013), “Values in Science beyond Underdetermination and Inductive Risk,” *Philosophy of Science* 80(5).
- Anderson (2004), “Uses of Value Judgments in Science,” *Hypatia* 19(1).
