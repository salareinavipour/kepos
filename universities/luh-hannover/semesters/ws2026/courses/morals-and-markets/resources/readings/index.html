    Reading list — Morals and Markets¶

    Background books¶

    • Hausman, Daniel M., Michael S. McPherson, and Debra Satz (2017), Economic Analysis, Moral Philosophy, and Public Policy, 3rd ed., Cambridge University Press. Chapters 6, 9, 10, 11 are assigned.
    • Reiss, Julian (2013), Philosophy of Economics: A Contemporary Introduction, Routledge. Chapter 13 is assigned.
    • Sen, Amartya (1988), On Ethics & Economics, Blackwell.

    Required readings¶

    • Mankiw (2021), Principles of Microeconomics, 9th ed., ch. 4.
    • Herzog (2025), “Markets,” Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (Fall 2025 ed.).
    • Polanyi (1944), The Great Transformation, chs. 4–6.
    • Friedman and Friedman (1980), Free to Choose, pp. 1–37.
    • Hayek (1945), “The Use of Knowledge in Society,” AER 35(4).
    • Satz (2008), “The Moral Limits of Markets: The Case of Human Kidneys,” PAS 108.
    • Bruni and Sugden (2013), “Reclaiming Virtue Ethics for Economics,” JEP 27(4).
    • Fumagalli (2020), “Buyer Beware,” J. Social Philosophy 51(3).
    • Angner (2023), How Economics Can Save the World, ch. 5.

    Background readings¶

    • North (1977), “Markets and Other Allocation Systems in History,” J. European Econ. History 6(3).
    • Coase (1960), “The Problem of Social Cost,” J. Law and Economics 3.
    • Anderson (1990), “The Ethical Limitations of the Market,” Economics and Philosophy 6.
    • Satz (1995), “Markets in Women’s Sexual Labor,” Ethics 106.
    • Tirole (2017), “The Moral Limits of the Market,” in Economics for the Common Good, pp. 33–61.
    • Hodgson (2021), “On the limits of markets,” J. Institutional Economics 17(1).
    • Satz (2010), Why Some Things Should Not Be for Sale, OUP.
